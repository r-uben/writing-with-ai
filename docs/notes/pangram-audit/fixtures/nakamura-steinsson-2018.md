# Concept: Nakamura & Steinsson (2018) — high-frequency monetary identification

**Source paper:** `2018__nakamura_steinsson__high_frequency_identification_of_monetary_non_neutrality_the_information_effect__QJE`
**Register:** empirical macro, identification strategy
**Target:** ~220 words, theory/identification prose (no new empirical results)

## Central claim

High-frequency identification around FOMC announcements cleanly separates monetary policy shocks from confounding macro news, but the shocks are small and their real-rate effects partly reflect an information channel: markets read rate moves as signals of the Fed's private information about the economy, not only as pure policy stance.

## Confirmed outline

1. Most interest-rate changes are endogenous; VAR controls leave residual bias — the identification problem is confounding, not missing variables alone.
2. The HF window fix: measure unexpected rate changes in ~30 minutes around scheduled FOMC releases; everything public before the window is already priced, so 9/11-type misattribution drops out.
3. Trade-off: identification is clean, power is not — shock SD ≈ 5 bp, too small to trace output quarters ahead; contemporaneous financial objects (TIPS, forwards, survey expectations) are where inference lives.
4. Punchline: policy news moves nominal and real rates at short horizons (e.g., ~106 bp on 2y real yields in their scaling) — markets believe monetary shocks matter for real rates — but long-run real effects fade, and part of the short-run move is an information effect (Fed revealing its assessment), not pure mechanical transmission.

## Supplied numbers (verify before use)

- 30-minute window around scheduled FOMC announcements
- Monetary shock SD ≈ 5 basis points
- 2-year real yield response ≈ 106 basis points (their scaling)
- 2-year nominal forward peak ≈ 114 basis points; 10-year forward can go negative on inflation channel
