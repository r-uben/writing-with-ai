# Concept: Fama & French (1997) — industry costs of equity are imprecise

**Source paper:** `1997__Fama_French__Industry_Costs_of_Equity__JFE`
**Register:** empirical finance / corporate finance
**Target:** ~220 words, methods + implication prose

## Central claim

Industry-level estimates of the cost of equity are far noisier than textbook practice assumes — standard errors above 3% per year are typical for both CAPM and the Fama-French three-factor model — because factor premia are uncertain and industry loadings are imprecisely estimated.

## Confirmed outline

1. Corporate finance textbooks stress cash-flow uncertainty; they understate that the discount rate itself is estimated with large error.
2. Model choice is unsettled: CAPM is standard but contested; FF3 fits better in sample but is empirically motivated — yet both produce fat uncertainty bands at the industry level.
3. Two error sources stack: (i) sampling error in estimated factor risk premia; (ii) imprecision in industry betas/loadings — neither is negligible.
4. Punchline: if industry costs of equity are measured with 3%+ SEs, firm- and project-level estimates are worse; precision in NPV analysis is partly illusory unless estimation risk enters the decision.
