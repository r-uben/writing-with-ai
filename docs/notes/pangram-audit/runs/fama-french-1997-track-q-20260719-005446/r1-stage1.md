Corporate finance texts train managers to fear forecast error in cash flows. Forecast error in the discount rate gets far less attention. Yet the rate is itself an estimate, and for industries a noisy one. Apply the CAPM to industry portfolios, or the three-factor model, and standard errors above 3.0% per year are typical — Fama and French document exactly that. Quote a "cost of equity" to one decimal place and you misstate what the data allow.

Two errors compound. Factor risk premia come from long historical samples and stay uncertain even after decades of data. Industry loadings on those factors carry error of their own — sometimes a lot — because industries lump firms into coarse buckets and betas drift as composition shifts. Switching models won't save you. The CAPM anchors the textbooks but describes the cross-section poorly; the three-factor model fits better in sample, though it grew from the data rather than from a tight utility foundation. Either way, the industry-level cost of equity comes with bands wide enough to swamp the spreads many projects assume.

Industries this imprecise are the favorable case. Single-firm and project-level estimates — shorter histories, noisier regressions — come out worse. Precision in NPV is partly a mirage until estimation risk enters the decision on its own terms.
