### Draft

On March 20, 2001, the FOMC cut the federal funds rate by a larger-than-expected 50 basis points. Within thirty minutes the S&P 500 fell. About one-third of FOMC announcements since 1990 show the same positive co-movement of rates and stocks. That pattern is the foothold: the high-frequency object called a monetary policy surprise already mixes a rate decision with news about beliefs.

Pure policy tightening should raise rates and lower equities—higher discount rates and weaker expected dividends. Negative co-movement of three-month fed funds futures and the S&P 500 in a half-hour window around FOMC announcements is the policy-shock signature; positive co-movement isolates a central-bank information shock. Jarociński and Karadi (2020) impose those high-frequency sign restrictions in a Bayesian SVAR. Purged policy shocks look more like textbook contractions, including a clearer price-level decline. Information shocks raise rates and raise activity and prices.

Independently, the Fed and markets often disagree about the path of rates—Greenbook assumptions and FOMC dots versus market forwards—partly because they disagree about future activity. In Caballero and Simsek (2022), opinionated demand disagreements make the market anticipate Fed “mistakes,” which move current demand and induce the Fed to partially accommodate the market and implement its own view only gradually. Announcements that reveal an unexpected change in the Fed’s belief microfound textbook-looking policy shocks. Tantrum shocks arise when the market misreads that belief and overreacts. Disagreement also warps market expected inflation and creates a cost-push-like trade-off.

Read together, the announcement window is a belief event as much as a rate event. Empirically, failing to purge information shocks biases inference on nonneutrality. Theoretically, disagreement and misinterpretation are why policy must be gradual and communicative even when the Fed optimizes under its own belief.

This note synthesizes identification and theory mechanisms from the two papers. It does not re-estimate either design or adjudicate which belief channel dominates in any subsample.
