## Draft principles P3–P5 (accept / revise / reject)

### P3 (limits): State the boundary as a hard result
- **Executable rule:** When evidence or identification imposes a material limit, state it in a direct independent clause; do not cushion it with “may,” “somewhat,” “should be interpreted with caution,” or praise for the estimate.
- **Good:** “This design identifies the impact response. It does not identify the long-run multiplier.”
- **Anti:** “While the estimates are informative, the long-run multiplier may be somewhat uncertain and should be interpreted with caution.”
- **Fits P1/P2:** The limit becomes the pivot rather than a retreat.

### P4 (openings): Begin with the observation that creates the problem
- **Executable rule:** Open an introduction or major section with a supplied dated, named, or quantified fact that exposes the mechanism; state the general question only after the fact has made it necessary. Never invent dates/numbers to satisfy this.
- **Good:** “On January 28, 2004, two- and five-year Treasury yields rose 20 and 25 basis points in the half-hour around the FOMC announcement. The funds-rate decision was fully anticipated.”
- **Anti:** “A central question in macroeconomics is how monetary policy affects the economy.”
- **Fits P1/P2:** Concrete phenomenon carries setup; sharp second sentence can land the turn (MS’s crisis open works the same way).

### P5 (citations): Make every citation do sentence-level work
- **Executable rule:** Introduce a source narratively when its method, finding, or disagreement advances the sentence’s claim. Do not use a multi-source parenthetical as a substitute for explaining the literature.
- **Good:** “Morris and Shin show that small private noise eliminates the multiplicity generated by common knowledge; their result makes the information structure, rather than sunspots, the object of the model.”
- **Anti:** “The role of information in currency crises is well established (Flood and Garber, 1984; Obstfeld, 1986, 1994, 1996; Morris and Shin, 1998).”
- **Fits P1/P2:** Assertive claim about each source’s role, then pivot to your contribution.
