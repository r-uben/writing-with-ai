### Draft

On March 20, 2001, the FOMC cut the federal funds rate by a larger-than-expected 50 basis points, yet the S&P 500 fell within thirty minutes. About one-third of FOMC announcements since 1990 show the same positive co-movement of rates and stocks. That pattern, documented by Jarociński and Karadi (2020), is the empirical foothold.

Pure policy tightening should raise rates and lower stocks—higher discount rates, weaker expected dividends. Negative rate–stock co-movement in the half-hour window around FOMC announcements, using three-month fed funds futures and the S&P 500, is the policy-shock signature. Positive co-movement is an information shock: the central bank reveals a private outlook. A Bayesian SVAR with those high-frequency sign restrictions separates the two. Purged policy shocks look more like textbook contractions, including a clearer price-level decline; information shocks raise rates and activity and prices together.

Caballero and Simsek (2022) start from a different belief margin. Greenbook assumptions and FOMC dots often diverge from market forwards, partly because the Fed and markets disagree about future activity. Opinionated demand disagreements make the market anticipate Fed “mistakes,” which shift current demand and induce the Fed to partially accommodate the market—implementing its own view only gradually. Announcements that reveal an unexpected change in the Fed’s belief microfound textbook-looking policy shocks. Tantrum shocks arise when the market misreads that belief and overreacts. Disagreement also warps market expected inflation, creating a cost-push-like trade-off. Gradualism and communication follow.

The announcement surprise is a belief event as much as a rate event. Empirically, failing to purge information shocks biases inference on nonneutrality. Theoretically, disagreement and misinterpretation are why optimal policy under the Fed’s own belief is still gradual and communicative.

This note synthesizes the two papers’ identification and theory mechanisms. It does not re-estimate either design, and it does not adjudicate which belief channel dominates in any subsample.
